AuctionDeposit

A safe way to put down a bid deposit at a property auction.

When a bank sells a repossessed home at auction, every bidder must first pay a deposit. Losers wait for their money back, auctions are often cancelled or suspended, and some winners never pay the rest. AuctionDeposit keeps every bidder's deposit in one escrow on GenLayer, and the money moves only on what the official auction record says:

  • losers get their deposit back;
  • the winner's deposit goes to the bank as part of the price once the record shows the price paid, or as a penalty if the winner walks away;
  • a cancelled or suspended auction refunds everyone.

Why it matters: nearly 300,000 property auctions are waiting in Greece, and only about 1 in 5 succeeds (auction listings: unsold properties are listed again; euro2day, citing IOBE and Cepal, Q1 2026, and the IMF). Europe's largest banks hold €373bn of bad loans (gross non-performing exposures of EU significant banks, Q2 2025, Banca Ifis).

The trust problem

At a forced sale, the deposit is the bidder's money held by someone else for weeks. The facts that decide where it should go (who won, whether the auction went ahead, whether the winner paid) are public on the auction platform's record, but on a page made for people. AuctionDeposit lets GenLayer's validators read that page, and plain code move the money.

How it works

1. Open

The bank freezes the terms: the auction and property as the record prints them, the record page (on a host frozen when the contract was deployed), the deposit, when deposits close, the winner's payment window and the refund deadline.

2. Deposit

Each bidder pays exactly the deposit and names the bidder code the record will use for them. A wrong amount is refused and credited back.

3. Read the record

Validators open the record page; the AI only copies the status, winner and balance lines. Code checks every copy against the page and a closed list of phrases. All validators must agree exactly.

4. Seen twice

Nothing moves on one look: a result counts once two readings, sent at least 10 minutes apart, show it. Losers are refunded as soon as the winner is confirmed.

5. Deadlines

No payment shown within the winner's window: its deposit goes to the bank. No result by the refund deadline: everyone is refunded. Code alone, 30 minutes late, no AI needed.

6. Credits

Every payout is a credit each party withdraws itself. After the runs below, every party withdrew and the contract held nothing.

Limits, stated plainly

  • The contract trusts one record page on a host fixed at deployment. In real life the record is kept by the auction platform (notaries or the state), not by the bank.
  • Anyone can open an auction for any auction number, so a bidder checks the bank's address before paying.
  • Bidder codes are linked to addresses only because bidders say so; taking someone's code first blocks them from the escrow but gains nothing.
  • The phrase lists are English: a record in Greek would read as unclear and refund everyone at the refund deadline.
